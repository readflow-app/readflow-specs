### DL-001 — "Self-hosted" definition
**Decision:** "Self-hosted" in ReadFlow's value proposition means the operator (builder) runs ReadFlow on her own AWS account. Users sign up on the operator's platform. Data does not leave the operator's AWS account. Users do not self-host their own instance.
**Rationale:** Target users (Vietnamese students) want assurance their reading data is not processed or stored by a foreign commercial SaaS (e.g., Vercel, US-only AI clouds). Self-hosting by end users is out of scope — too high a DevOps burden for the target audience, and not the intent.
**Status:** Captured in NFR-DATA section of PRD.

---

### DL-002 — v1 revenue model: free-only
**Decision:** v1 is entirely free-tier. No paid subscription in v1 scope. One paid tier is planned but deferred until the core read → note loop is validated with real users.
**Rationale:** v1 goal is learning and validation, not revenue. Adding payments increases engineering scope and introduces pricing pressure that could distort product decisions before product-market fit is established.
**Status:** Captured in Scope section (v1) and Goals section.

---

### DL-003 — v1 MVP scope trim: flashcards deferred to v1.5
**Decision:** Flashcard generation and SM-2 spaced repetition are deferred from v1 to v1.5. v1 scope = auth + PDF upload/render + AI bilingual explain + auto smart note + paper library + quota enforcement.
**Rationale:** Total build capacity for 6-week MVP is approximately 12–24 hours (solo developer, 2–4 hrs/week). Original must-have list was over-ambitious for this capacity. The core hypothesis to validate is the read → note loop (comprehension + knowledge capture), not the retention loop (flashcard review). Flashcards validate a distinct hypothesis and can follow once reading is validated.
**Tradeoff noted:** SM-2/flashcards are a high-value differentiator vs. RemNote. Deferral risks users finding RemNote adequate once they have smart notes. Mitigated by making v1.5 timeline fast and communicating the roadmap in UI.
**Status:** Captured in Scope section. Known tradeoff documented.

---

### DL-004 — AI model tier split
**Decision:** Claude Haiku via AWS Bedrock for inline explain calls; Claude Sonnet via AWS Bedrock for smart note generation (and future flashcard generation).
**Rationale:** Cost-quality tradeoff. Explain is called up to 50 times per user per day — latency and cost must be minimized. Smart note is generated at most 5 times per user per month — quality and structure matter more than cost or latency at this frequency.
**Status:** Captured in FR-026 (explain) and FR-036 (smart note).

---

### DL-005 — v1 PDF scope: text-based only
**Decision:** v1 supports text-based PDFs only. Scanned/image PDFs, LaTeX equation rendering, and multi-column layout optimization are explicitly out of scope.
**Rationale:** PDF.js handling of complex academic formats (LaTeX, multi-column) is a high-risk engineering rabbit hole disproportionate to the v1 build budget. The primary user reads standard research papers and ArXiv preprints — most are text-based PDFs. Scoped out with clear user-facing error messages to prevent silent failure.
**Status:** Captured in NFR-PDF section and Known Limitations table.

---

### DL-006 — AI explain acceptance criteria
**Decision:** An AI explanation is acceptable if a Vietnamese reader with intermediate English proficiency can understand the technical term without opening another browser tab. A literal dictionary translation with no technical context does not meet this standard.
**Rationale:** The core value proposition of ReadFlow is "understand without tab-switching." If the explanation does not meet this bar, the product fails its own promise. This is a product quality standard, not just a nice-to-have.
**Status:** Captured in FR-023. Must be used as evaluation criterion in QA and prompt engineering.

---

### DL-007 — Free tier quota limits
**Decision:** Free tier = 50 AI explain calls per day (resets UTC 00:00) + 5 smart note generations per month (resets on account anniversary).
**Rationale:** Based on AWS Bedrock Haiku pricing and target < $0.50/active user/month constraint. 50 explain calls/day × Haiku cost per call ≈ well within budget at realistic usage. 5 smart notes/month × Sonnet cost per call ≈ $0.05–0.15 per user/month depending on paper length.
**Status:** Captured in FR-050 through FR-054.

---

### DL-008 — Smart note structure
**Decision:** Smart note format is fixed: What / Why / How / Key Terms (5–10 bilingual definitions). Not user-configurable in v1.
**Rationale:** Fixed format creates consistency that enables future flashcard generation (key terms are already extracted). Reduces prompt complexity. User can always export and reformat downstream (v1.5: export to Markdown/Notion).
**Status:** Captured in FR-033.

---

---

### DL-009 — Smart note trigger: banner + explicit confirm
**Decision:** When user reaches the last page, show a non-intrusive banner: "You've finished this paper. Generate smart note?" Confirm/Dismiss. Never auto-generate. If dismissed, banner does not reappear in session; manual trigger always available.
**Rationale:** User may not be done reading when they reach the last page — they may scroll back to re-read sections. Auto-generation would create unwanted AI calls and surprise the user. Explicit confirmation preserves user intent and controls Bedrock cost.
**Status:** Captured in FR-030, FR-030a, FR-031.

---

### DL-010 — Reading position saved server-side in v1
**Decision:** Reading position (last viewed page) is included in v1, saved server-side. Local browser state (localStorage) is not acceptable. Cross-device sync optimization deferred to v1.5.
**Rationale:** User must be able to close browser and return to the same page. Browser state is too fragile (cleared caches, private browsing). Server-side is the minimum reliable implementation.
**Status:** Captured in FR-014.

---

### DL-011 — Explanation persistence: ephemeral default + explicit save
**Decision:** Explanations are ephemeral by default. A "Save explanation" button in the popup lets the user attach the explanation to the text position. Saved explanations are viewable on return. Full persistent highlights (color-coded, managed UI) remain v1.5.
**Rationale:** Keeps default cost low (ephemeral = no storage per call). Power users get an escape hatch without requiring a full highlights system. Avoids building highlights management in v1.
**Status:** Captured in FR-025, FR-025a, FR-025b.

---

### DL-012 — Smart note: AI section read-only + editable My Notes block
**Decision:** AI-generated What/Why/How/Key Terms is read-only. An editable "My Notes" freeform block sits below it — auto-saved, visually distinct.
**Rationale:** Prevents accidental corruption of AI output. Gives users a place for their own commentary without mixing it with structured AI content. Clean separation enables future features (export, search) that can treat AI and user content differently.
**Status:** Captured in FR-035, FR-035a.

---

### DL-013 — Privacy disclosure: one-line on upload screen
**Decision:** A one-line disclosure appears on the upload screen: "Paper content is processed by AWS Bedrock (Claude) in a controlled AWS environment. Your data is not used to train AI models." No full consent flow or privacy policy required in v1.
**Rationale:** Disclosure is proportionate to the dogfooding / validation phase. Full privacy policy is a pre-public-launch requirement (noted in account deletion decision below). One-line covers the reasonable expectation of users who signed up knowing it's a controlled-hosting product.
**Status:** Captured in FR-037.

---

### DL-014 — Account deletion: manual for dogfooding, self-serve before public launch
**Decision:** v1 (dogfooding phase) handles account deletion via admin action. Self-serve account deletion is required before public launch but is not in v1 scope.
**Rationale:** During solo dogfooding, admin deletion is sufficient. Public launch requires self-serve deletion for basic user trust and potential data-regulation compliance.
**Status:** Pre-launch checklist item. Not in PRD FRs until confirmed for a future sprint.

---

### DL-015 — Domain tag: inferred automatically
**Decision:** Domain tag in AI explain (Security / DevOps / General) is inferred from paper content/metadata, not set by user on upload.
**Rationale:** Reduces friction at upload. Users uploading a security paper should not have to label it — the AI model has enough context to infer this. Inferred tag can always be wrong; this is acceptable in v1.
**Status:** Captured in FR-022 behavior. Prompt engineering implication for AI explain.
